# Engine2 audit: what EmberEngine reuses, adapts, and still lacks (2026-10-14)

Read-only review of `C:\Engine2` (its live battle path: `src/game/Battle3D.tsx` → `DioramaView`
(`src/editor/ember/dioramaView.ts`) → `Stage` (`src/render/stage.ts`); `ThreeBattleRenderer.ts` is the
older renderer, kept only for shared helpers). Engine2 and its assets stay untouched. EmberEngine makes
its own versions: cooked copies of the assets, C++/Vulkan ports of the logic.

**Rule (the user, 2026-10-14):** Engine2's game design is sound and is ported faithfully. Its engine is
what failed: it **stutters on spell effects** and its **sprite animation is mostly subpar**. For D1 every
asset is Engine2's real content wherever its format and rendering needs allow; nothing is a stand-in.
Porting this content validates the renderer. Whether proxy LOD is needed is a separate question, settled
later by a representative final-quality building (§7.2 of ARCHITECTURE.md).

Legend: **Reuse** = the asset or data goes in as it is (cooked, never edited); **Adapt** = Engine2's
behaviour is re-implemented natively (C++ / HLSL / Vulkan), same rules and look; **Missing** = EmberEngine
has nothing for it yet.

---

## 1. Terrain: hexes, elevation, ground textures, variants, cliffs, blending

**Engine2:** `map/board.ts` (cells from the map: terrain, level, groundY; Ember's elevation rule
`max(authored, terrain minimum)`, `STEP = 1` world unit per level, base 2 levels; mountains turn to rock
at level 7, snow at 11), `render/terrainMesh.ts` (the two looks: **smooth** = dense heightfield, ~220k
cells max, hex lookup through an fbm domain warp of 1.25 so terraces wander; **hard** = a crisp cliff
quad at every height step; exact cut at void hexes with a 0.35 lip; wall-aware footprint), per-vertex
**splat weights** from a 7-sample ring (8 base surfaces + 40 baked ground layers), `render/terrainMaterial.ts`
(one material: world-space UVs at 4 units per repeat, grass with Quilez no-tile sampling, cliffs
side-projected from x/z at 2 units, earth vs rock cliff by surface family, set layers blended with their
normal and roughness maps on flat tops), `render/groundSets.ts` (which folder feeds each layer),
`render/textures.ts` (9 procedural 256 px fallback surfaces: grass, forest, pavers, snow, rock, gravel,
ash, earth, cliff), `render/terrainRelief.ts` (real vertex relief for the photo layers, from
`relief.json` height grids), `ember/tileVariants.ts` (append-only variant numbering that saved maps rely on),
`core/noise.ts`, `core/hex.ts` (pointy-top, odd rows shifted), `render/meadowGrass.ts` (real 3D grass
blades on the meadow variant).

**Assets:** `public/game/ground-claude` (15 sets, colour/normal/rough), `ground-engine2-v3`,
`ground-engine2-v4` (13 photoreal sets incl. city, shallow water, river rocks), `ground-variant-tiles`,
`ground-variants-3d` (17 normal/rough/relief sets): ~290 MB of PNG.

| | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ground texture sets, relief grids | **Reuse** | Cooked to BC7 (colour), BC5 (normal), BC4/BC7 (roughness) at **source resolution**. Engine2 squeezed every layer to 1024² to fit one WebGL texture array; bindless needs no common size. |
| 243 maps (`C:\Engine2\maps`) | **Reuse** | Read as they are (JSON: tiles, tileVariants, terrainElevations, decorations, spawns, lighting fields). |
| Board rules, variant → layer tables | **Adapt** | Port `board.ts` + `tileVariants.ts` logic exactly (saved indices must keep their art). |
| Smooth / hard ground builders | **Adapt** | C++ port of `terrainMesh.ts`, same noise and constants; verified vertex-for-vertex against Engine2's own output (exported by running its TypeScript read-only). |
| Procedural fallback surfaces | **Adapt** | Generated by the cooker with the same noise code, same 256 px tiles. |
| Terrain material | **Adapt** | HLSL port of the GLSL in `terrainMaterial.ts`. Weights packed per map (only the layers a map uses), interpolated like Engine2's. |
| Meadow 3D grass blades | **Adapt** | Instanced on the GPU instead of one mesh per patch. |
| Terrain in EmberEngine today | **Missing** | Only a flat placeholder plane exists (`shaders/ground.hlsl`). M4's streamed clipmap terrain is later; D1 needs the board as Engine2 builds it. |

## 2. Water

**Engine2:** `buildWater` in `terrainMesh.ts` (one sheet that follows the shore of the active look,
water level per cell `(2 - 0.35 + level)`, rises to meet banks, hidden under dry land), water material
with a generated ripple normal map (`textures.ts`), `ThreeWater.ts` (older path, brush footprints).

| | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Water sheet geometry | **Adapt** | C++ port, same rules. |
| Water shading (ripple normal, colour, shore) | **Adapt** | Port; transparency needs a forward pass after the opaque world. |
| Water in EmberEngine | **Missing** | No transparent world pass yet. |

## 3. Props, decor, architecture placement

**Engine2:** `world/decor.ts` (each decoration is an upright photoreal card at full native resolution,
Ember's sizes (`decorSize`), anchored at the footprint centroid with the art's opaque base on the ground;
side files and mirroring by `decorationFacing`; experiment of 2026-10-13: cards stand square to the hex
rows instead of turning with the camera), `ember/data.ts` `DECORATIONS` (every decoration's footprint,
model, light, wall style), 3D props (`propModel`, 15 GLBs in `public/game/models`), 3D trees, and
**procedural 3D architecture** (`ThreeWalls.ts`, `ThreeCastle.ts`: walls, doors, castle and temple pieces
built in code and textured with the photographs in `public/game/textures/walls|doors`).

**Assets:** 443 decoration images (434 MB), `props/` (98 MB, incl. the gothic cottages), `models/` (11 GLBs; with the 4 cottages in `props/`, the 15 M3 cooked).

| | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Decoration images | **Reuse** | Already cooked as cards (M3 battle pack, full resolution, BC7, streamed). |
| GLB props, trees, cottages | **Reuse** | Already cooked (M3 meshes, cluster LOD). |
| `DECORATIONS` table, sizes, anchors, facing | **Adapt** | Exported from Engine2's own TypeScript into JSON data that EmberEngine reads, plus a C++ port of the sizing/anchoring rules. |
| Wall/door/castle/temple builder | **Adapt** | C++ port of the geometry code; its photographs cooked as textures. |
| Placing a map's decorations in EmberEngine | **Missing** | The battle test scatters decor at random; nothing reads a map yet. |

## 4. Character sprites and animation

**Engine2:** `units/frames.ts` (trimmed full-resolution PNG frames, loaded on demand, LRU budget),
`units/catalog.ts` (every Ember class, poses, frame prefixes, body-centre offsets), `units/actor.ts`
(camera-facing card, Ember's sizes and timings, walk directions, authored left cuts, no mirroring while
moving). **Assets:** `sprites-e2` 8,127 frames (1.9 GB), `sprites` 9,914 (1.7 GB), `sprites-clean`, 72
portraits.

| | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sprite frames | **Reuse** | Already cooked (M2: 375 clips, BC7, per-clip residency; ×10 tested). |
| Catalog, timings, facing rules, centring | **Adapt** | Exported data + C++ actor logic. |
| Animation quality | **Engine2's weak point** | Fixed 36 frames per pose (a 4.75 s idle plays at ~7.6 fps), per-frame cut-outs (edge shimmer), recentred bodies sliding at an unrelated speed (feet glide). Fixed by the video → sprite pipeline (ARCHITECTURE §5), which waits on the organised videos. D1 uses today's frames, played at their authored timing. |

## 5. Lighting, shadows, time of day

**Engine2:** `render/stage.ts`: one sun with a 4096² PCF shadow map fitted to the board, hemisphere
sky/ground light, **time-of-day presets** (day, noon, dawn, dusk, bright and dark night; nights lit by the
moon with its phase from `moonPhase.ts`, blood moon every third cycle), neutral tonemapping, bloom
(0.3 strength, 0.55 radius, threshold 2.0), optional tilt-shift, neutral grade with a soft vignette;
point lights padded to a fixed budget so three.js never recompiles mid-battle. `vfx/lighting.ts` +
`render/carriedLight.ts`: light-emitting decorations (braziers, lanterns, burning houses) and carried
torches as real point lights with a softened falloff. `units/spriteMaterial.ts`: **how cards are lit**:
shading normal facing the camera tilted 29° upward, wrap-around falloff, one exposure calibrated so
daylight shows the art as painted, a readability floor at night, flame pixels glow past bloom, a cap on
over-brightening near lights, unit cards pulled slightly toward the camera to win over props in their hex.

| | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Time-of-day and moon presets, light colours and intensities | **Reuse** (data) | Exported as data; same numbers. |
| Map lighting fields (`timeOfDay`, `sunIntensity`, `ambientIntensity`, `bloomIntensity`, …) | **Reuse** | Read from the map. |
| Sun shadow map, point-light shadows | **Adapt** | Native shadow maps (cascaded or one fitted map first, to match), alpha-tested card casters. |
| Light-emitting decorations, carried lights, falloff | **Adapt** | Clustered light list instead of three.js's fixed light count, so lights never recompile anything. |
| Card lighting model (`spriteMaterial.ts`) | **Adapt**, as D1's baseline | The starting point for §7.1's comparison; D1 measures it against the alternatives. |
| Bloom, tonemap, vignette, tilt-shift | **Adapt** | EmberEngine has a tonemap pass only. |
| Lights, shadows, card lighting in EmberEngine | **Missing** | Sprites are unlit; meshes have a provisional sun + sky in the resolve. |

## 6. Spell effects (where Engine2 stutters)

**Engine2:** the rules engine (`game/engine.ts`, 14k lines) emits effect events; effects come from three
families: three.js particle systems (`vfx/legacy/three/*`: Fireball, Fireball impact, Caustic Venom, Bless,
Phantasmal Force, Magic Missile V2, Burning Hands V2/V3, Varredura/Cleave, Web of Dreams, element
flipbook emitters, ~4k lines), Ember's **2D canvas/WebGL effects** (`vfx/legacy/two-d/*`: lightning,
Turn Undead V1–V4, Provoke, Frost, Blizzard) drawn into a **screen-sized canvas that is uploaded and laid
over every frame** (`stage.ts` DepthOverlayShader, `battleSpellOverlay3d.ts`), arrows and bolts
(`battleProjectiles3d.ts`), spell point lights. **Assets:** `public/game/fx` 95 flipbook PNGs (108 MB).

Likely causes of the stutter, **to be measured, not assumed**: a full-screen canvas re-uploaded to the GPU
each frame while effects play; shader programs compiled on first use (a spell's first cast); new lights
changing the light count; CPU-side particle updates on the main thread.

| | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Flipbook and effect images | **Reuse** | Cooked as texture arrays (one bindless index per flipbook). |
| Each spell's look, timing and parameters | **Adapt** | Ported spell by spell onto one GPU particle system (simulation in compute, no per-frame uploads, every pipeline built at load). The 2D canvas effects become world-space or screen-space GPU effects with the same art. |
| Effect lights | **Adapt** | Into the clustered light list (R4: effects emit real light). |
| VFX system in EmberEngine | **Missing** | Nothing yet. D1 ports one spell end to end (with its light), D3 the rest. |

## 7. Fog of war, mist, atmosphere

**Engine2:** `render/fogOfWar.ts`: Ember's rules and look from per-hex sight (**never seen = opaque
black, seen before = 60% dark, in sight = clear**, soft edge only inside remembered hexes, 8 mask texels
per hex radius), drawn as a darkened copy of the terrain surfaces so cliffs and walls are covered;
decoration cards in fog darkened or hidden; hidden enemies not drawn. `render/volumetricFog.ts`:
"Névoa 3D", a half-resolution ray-marched ground mist in world space (pools in valleys, units stand in
it, lights scatter in it, drifts), with Ember's mist presets (`game/mist3d.ts`). `ThreeAtmosphere` wisps,
CSS screen vignettes (`BattleVignettes.tsx`).

| | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sight rules, three states, darkness values | **Reuse** (rules) | Same numbers. |
| The mask and how it is applied | **Adapt**, upgraded to R7 | One visibility mask that **every** pass reads (lights, shadows, VFX, mist, cards), instead of a darkened overlay: lights from visible areas cannot reveal hidden ground, unexplored stays black even under lit fog. |
| Volumetric ground mist and its presets | **Adapt** | Froxel volume (lit by the clustered lights and shadow maps) fed by the same mask (R7's literal fog along the boundary). |
| Wisps, vignettes | **Adapt** | GPU particles; screen-space vignette in post. |
| All of it in EmberEngine | **Missing** | |

## 8. Materials, shaders, asset loading

**Engine2:** three.js materials patched with GLSL (`onBeforeCompile`) plus TSL copies for WebGPU;
`MeshStandardMaterial` PBR for GLBs; assets fetched as PNG/JSON/GLB from the dev server, decoded in the
browser (`createImageBitmap`), uploaded raw (RGBA8), some packed at load into texture arrays.

| | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| PNG / GLB / JSON sources | **Reuse** | Cooked once into the pak (BC7/BC5, cluster-LOD meshes); never decoded at runtime. |
| Shaders | **Adapt** | HLSL (DXC, SPIR-V), every pipeline created at load: nothing compiles mid-game. |
| Loading | Already native | Async IO, archive, per-asset streaming (M1/M2). |

## What EmberEngine already covers

Async IO and the archive (M1); sprite cooking and residency at ×10 (M2); GPU-driven 3D meshes with cluster
LOD and occlusion culling, Engine2's 15 GLBs and 443 decorations cooked, 28k cards and 26k pieces measured
in the battle test (M3).

## D1 port order (each step on Engine2's real content, compared side by side with Engine2)

1. Export Engine2's data tables (decorations, terrain, variants, sprite catalog, lighting presets) by
   running its TypeScript read-only; read one map.
2. Board + both ground looks + the ground material, on the cooked ground sets; verified against Engine2's
   own geometry and a capture of the same map from the same camera.
3. Water; decorations, props and architecture placed from the map.
4. Units from the spawns, with their poses and timings.
5. Sun shadows, clustered point lights (decor lights, carried lights), card lighting (Engine2's model as the
   baseline), bloom and grade, time-of-day presets.
6. Fog of war mask read by every pass (R7) and the volumetric mist.
7. One spell end to end on the GPU particle system, with its light: no stutter, measured frame by frame.
